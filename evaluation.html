Without Ground Truth

Measures of quality that need no ground truth, so they work on a real world as well as a synthetic one. The first set is the most telling: it checks forecasts against what came later. The rest check how well the model fits the data it has used, which is a weaker test, since a model always looks better on data it has seen.

Forecasts against later estimates

At each run date of the backtest (), the model made a nowcast and a year of forecasts. A year later the next run, with a year more observations, estimated the same months again. That later estimate is not the truth, but it is better informed, so a good forecast should land near it: the later estimate should fall inside the earlier 90% band about 90% of the time, and the revision should be small. Bands are as published, after widening.

Observations against the model

Each observation against the model's prediction of it, plus the observation's own noise. About 90% should fall inside the 90% band, and the typical gap, in units of the band's spread (|z|), should be near 0.67. Lower is not better here: the filter has already used these observations, so a very close fit can mean the model chases noise. Yes/no observations (satellite activity) are not scored.

By sector

Sector totals against official statistics

Where the sector input bundle carries an official statistic that measures a sector total, the model's total (converted into the statistic's units) should agree with it within the band of their gap. The filter uses these statistics too (L16), so a large or persistent gap means the model's entities cannot reproduce the official picture.

What the data pin down

How much the data narrowed what the model knew before seeing them. For entity parameters, the share whose uncertainty the fit cut by at least a fifth (sd ratio below 0.8). For sector totals, the quantities whose estimate is still mostly prior (posterior over prior variance above 0.8): no observation reaches them, so the equations alone carry them.
