Firms behind the sector totals

Model 2 estimates hidden monthly quantities for individual operations (steel mills, airline networks, data-center campuses): capacity, how busy they are, order books, prices. It works from scattered reports such as company filings, emissions, satellite readings and traffic statistics. Establishments without their own data are covered by one representative entity per sector, size class and region. The operations are summed to sector totals and checked against Model 1's sector nowcasts.

This month, sector by sector

Where to look

  • Sectors: every sector total over time, with its 90% band, the truth, the split between tracked operations and representative entities, and the check against Model 1.
  • Operations: one page per operation. Its hidden states, each observation against what the model predicted for it, and its fitted parameters against prior and truth.
  • Evidence: every observation type, where it comes from, how often and how late it arrives, how well the model fits it, and what nothing observes yet.
  • Accuracy: the backtest's errors and band coverage by horizon, the band widening, and how well estimation recovers the true parameters.

Known problems

  • The model's own 90% bands are too narrow: in the backtest they held the truth 66% of the time across all sector variables (52% for the five main ones). The published sector totals are widened by calibrated factors (PLAN decision 17). Entity charts show the filter's own, unwidened spread.
  • Data centers: normal occupancy is fitted low (0.74 vs 0.86) and nothing observes it directly. Rent churn (spec 0.7.5) fixed most of the lease-term error (81 vs 84 months, was 75), which halved the shipments error.
  • Data centers: at single sites the order book sits well above the truth even though company backlog reports fit; the company total is split across its sites wrongly.
  • Steel: the order book runs high, about 8% at the nowcast.
  • Steel: finished-goods stock runs 4–5 times the truth (truth inside the band 1% of months). No observation covers it, so it is set by the prior.
  • Lead time runs about 12% low at the sector level for steel and data centers (data centers: truth inside the band 0% of months). Lead-time reports carry a per-source bias the fit can absorb.
