Firms behind the sector totals

Model 2 estimates monthly quantities for the entities in a sector: capacity, output, order books, inventories and prices. It covers one sector for each of Model 1's 13 industries, plus data centers: steel mills, oil and gas fields, auto plants, homebuilders, retail chains, chip fabs, chemical plants, food plants, hospitals, hotels, machinery plants, wholesalers, airline networks and data-center campuses. It learns from scattered observations: company filings, emissions, satellite readings, permits and traffic statistics. Model 2 sums the entities to sector totals and checks the totals against Model 1's sector nowcasts.

Entities come in two kinds. Tracked entities are specific sites with observations of their own, and the model follows each one separately. Representative entities stand in for all the other establishments, which have no observations of their own: one per sector, size class and region, scaled to the establishment count in Census County Business Patterns. For example, steel has 14 tracked mills. One of them, Acme Gary Works, has 358 observations: revenue and order book from its company's filings, emissions, satellite images, stated capacity, list prices and lead times. The representative entity Steel: medium, midwest stands for 10 medium-sized midwestern establishments together and has 7 observations: Census employment and Census shipments for the whole group. The steel total is the 14 tracked mills plus 11 representative entities.

Everything on this site is simulated. A synthetic world runs Model 2's own equations with known true parameters to produce a monthly history for every entity, and then generates observations from that history, with noise and realistic publication delays. The entities and companies are invented (Acme, CloudFort, Vertex): their sectors, sizes, regions, data sources and reporting schedules imitate real ones, but no figure comes from a real firm. Model 1's input is synthetic too. Model 2 sees only the observations, and the site compares its estimates with the hidden truth (orange). A good match shows that the estimation recovers what the data contain; it does not show that the equations describe real firms. That test needs real data: the first real series (data-center order books from REIT filings) is downloaded but not yet used.

Known problems

  • All sectors: 90% bands miss the truth too often. Unwidened, they hold it 66% of the time across all sector quantities (52% for capacity, output, utilization, order book and shipments). Sector totals use widened bands (PLAN decision 17); entity charts use unwidened bands.
  • Steel: the order book runs about 8% high at the nowcast.
  • Steel: finished inventory runs 4–5 times the truth (in band 1% of months). No observation covers it, so the prior sets it.
  • Steel and data centers: lead time runs about 12% low (data centers: in band 0% of months). Lead-time observations carry a per-source bias that can absorb the error.
  • Data centers: normal occupancy fits low (fitted 0.74, true 0.86). No observation covers it directly.
  • Data centers: site order books run well above the truth, although company order-book observations fit. The model splits each company's order book across its sites wrongly.
