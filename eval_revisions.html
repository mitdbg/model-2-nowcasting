Forecasts against later estimates

A forecast can be checked without knowing the truth: wait, and see what the model says about the same months once more data has come in. At each run date of the backtest the model forecast a year ahead; a year later the next run estimated those months again, with a year more observations. That later estimate is not the truth, only a better-informed guess, but a good forecast should land close to it.

Sector by sector

The same two questions for each sector and each quantity. A well-sized range catches about 90 of every 100 later estimates: fewer, and the range is too narrow (the model is overconfident); many more, and it is wider than it needs to be.
