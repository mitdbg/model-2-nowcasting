Evidence: observations and their sources

Observations are everything the model learns from outside data. Each observation is about one subject: an entity, or a grouping of entities such as a company, a site or a Census cell. It informs the hidden states of the entities it covers (§6.6), and through estimation their parameters. Model 1's sector series enter separately, as the check on each sector page. OBSERVATION_SOURCES.md holds the full written inventory.

Observation types

  • Observations: count published by the as-of date across the whole synthetic history.
  • In window: count attached to this run.
  • Subjects: count of distinct entities and groupings the type's observations are about.
  • Lag: median days from the end of the period covered to publication.
  • In 90% band: share of observations inside the model's 90% predictive band. Near 90% is right; well above means σ is too large.
  • Innovation variance: variance of the filter's standardized innovations. Near 1 is right; above 1 means σ is too small or the model is biased for that type.

What each type describes

  • About: the subjects an observation of the type may name. An observation about any other kind is not used.
  • Covers: which members of the subject the observation counts. Census counts leave out the tracked entities.
  • Needs: modules every covered entity's template must have. Rent churn needs leases, for example.
  • Combined as: how the covered entities' values become one predicted value.
